# Coursework Draft

## Frequency-domain representations for experimental detection of AI-generated images

**Research snapshot:** 2 September 2026; **status:** evidence-bound final research draft. Only results
marked *observed* have completed metrics; in-progress or pending results are not imputed,
estimated, or described as completed.

> **Scope notice.** The historical Defactify pilot differentiates photographs from images generated
> by five named systems under specified benchmark conditions; HighRes-v1 will have a separately
> frozen source composition. Neither study authenticates an image, establishes its provenance,
> identifies an author, detects face/video manipulations, or proves that an arbitrary image was
> generated by AI.

## Annotation

This coursework investigates the limits of experimental classification of real photographs and
AI-generated images. Its historical low-resolution pilot uses the 96,000-image Defactify benchmark,
containing 16,000 real photographs and 16,000 images from each of Stable Diffusion 2.1, SDXL, SD 3,
DALL-E 3, and Midjourney v6. A provenance audit found that the official split contains caption and
exact-pHash overlap. More importantly, all synthetic records are square while the real photographs
have varied geometry. A metadata-only control therefore achieved ROC-AUC 0.980724 and balanced
accuracy 0.975833, demonstrating a major dataset shortcut rather than a useful detector.

The historical protocol was consequently amended before neural experiments. Every H1-N pixel model
received a common source-normalised raster: RGB decoding, square crop without padding, and one
128 x 128 LANCZOS resize; FFT features were calculated only after this transformation. A
group-disjoint 70/15/15 split kept the known normalized-caption/group-ID, exact-SHA-256, and
exact-pHash components in one partition.
On the resulting exploratory internal test, a controlled 64-bin radial-spectrum logistic-regression
baseline obtained ROC-AUC 0.708864, balanced accuracy 0.653833, and macro-F1 0.587577. Its result
varied materially by generator, with DALL-E 3 close to chance by balanced accuracy (0.530625).

One RGB ResNet-50 trained from random initialisation completed under the recorded historical
protocol on Apple MPS. Its one-seed exploratory internal result was ROC-AUC 0.887956, balanced
accuracy 0.802917, macro-F1 0.704555, and FPR@TPR95 0.482500. It is not a completed RGB-versus-FFT
comparison, a model-selection candidate, external evidence, or a high-resolution result.

The primary HighRes-v1 study is frozen around a common 384 x 384 raster from DANI [14]. The
selected local corpus has 7,410 exact 1024 x 1024 images from 1,482 COCO parent groups: one real
COCO photograph and four generated conditions per parent. Whole parents form train, validation,
and internal-test partitions. The original-byte audit found no exact duplicate, cross-label exact
duplicate, cross-parent pHash link at distance at most four, or cross-split component, but it did
find label-dependent JPEG/PNG and L/RGB support. The source rasters were therefore converted to a
separate 1024 x 1024 RGB PNG corpus without resizing, with source-to-derived hashes retained. A
fresh audit passed all training gates.

A logistic control using only geometry, encoded size, container, and colour mode obtained
validation ROC-AUC 0.713427 on the original files and 0.537373 on the canonical RGB PNG files.
This is observed evidence that acquisition metadata are informative and that canonicalisation
removes most, but not necessarily all, of that simple shortcut. The predeclared matched ResNet-50
comparison then completed on validation only for RGB and FFT from random initialisation over seeds
7, 17, and 42. FFT was selected by mean validation balanced accuracy (0.787556 versus 0.657138),
while seed 17 was used as the representative checkpoint by a rule declared before test inspection.
On the subsequently opened 1,110-image parent-disjoint internal test, the frozen FFT checkpoint
obtained ROC-AUC 0.893941, balanced accuracy 0.826014, and macro-F1 0.774670. Its group-bootstrap
95% ROC-AUC interval was 0.871820--0.916324. Robustness testing produced important negative
evidence: balanced accuracy fell to 0.518581 at JPEG quality 50, 0.476914 after 0.75 resizing,
0.478604 after 0.50 resizing, and 0.453829 under Gaussian blur. No post-test tuning was performed.
External Synthbuster + RAISE evaluation remains unavailable, so the local prototype remains gated
and must not present the score as a probability or proof.

A separate Defactify native384 sensitivity audit was materialised only to test whether canonical
output geometry and upstream-role preservation remove the observed source shortcut. It retained
16,328 rows after excluding 36 cross-role leakage components and verified every retained PNG by
byte and decoded-pixel hash. The non-pixel file-size control nevertheless reached ROC-AUC 0.650114
and balanced accuracy 0.595238 on the locked upstream test role. Since all final inputs have the
same 384 x 384 geometry, this is residual PNG/source-pipeline association rather than a detector
result. No neural model was trained from this corpus; it is recorded as negative evidence that a
better matched primary source remains necessary.

# 1 Introduction

## 1.1 Problem statement

Text-to-image systems can create convincing pictures of ordinary scenes at low cost. This makes
image-forensics tools attractive for research, platform moderation, and journalistic triage. At the
same time, the technical problem is often stated too broadly. A classifier trained to separate two
particular collections may learn the image source, encoder, resolution, compression history, prompt
template, or other properties of the benchmark rather than a stable trace of synthesis. Its high
test score can then be mistaken for evidence of image provenance.

The present work treats this risk as a central research question rather than as a minor
implementation detail. The project investigates still images of general scenes, not faces or video.
The target label is *real photograph* versus *synthetic image from a declared generator family*.
It is a benchmark label, not a verdict about the history of an uploaded file. The desired academic
outcome is a reproducible and falsifiable answer to a narrow representation question, including the
possibility that the frequency-domain approach does not generalise.

## 1.2 Aim, object, subject, and tasks

The **aim** is to document the limitations of the controlled 128 x 128 Defactify pilot and to
construct a separately auditable high-resolution study in which image representations can be tested
without silently upscaling or relabelling the historical result.

The **object of study** is a collection of real and AI-generated images with documented labels.
The **subject of study** is the relationship between the image representation, the experimental
data partition, and the observed performance of binary classifiers.

To achieve the aim, the following tasks were defined:

1. Acquire and document an image corpus with named real and synthetic sources.
2. Audit labels, sizes, captions, hashes, and cross-split overlap before interpreting a benchmark
   result.
3. Construct a leakage-resistant, generator-balanced grouped split.
4. Establish diagnostic controls that can reveal a source or geometry shortcut.
5. Report the historical radial FFT and one recorded RGB ResNet-50 pilot without converting either
   into a completed representation comparison.
6. Quantify uncertainty by resampling whole content/leakage groups, rather than treating correlated
   image rows as independent observations.
7. Materialise a fixed HighRes-v1 corpus only after a metadata audit, then construct its
   leakage-aware split and pre-register training and selection rules.
8. After the HighRes-v1 design is frozen, evaluate internal performance, deterministic JPEG, resize,
   and blur transformations, and transfer to a separately held-out external source.
9. Provide a local prototype only after a HighRes-v1 checkpoint has been selected by the research
   process, externally validated, and described as a model score rather than a provenance claim.

## 1.3 Research questions and hypotheses

The primary HighRes-v1 question is:

> Under a common, source-normalised 384 x 384 raster made from fixed-quality source images of at
> least 512 pixels, what evidence remains that a classifier distinguishes the specified real-photo
> and generated-image distributions internally and on locked external tests?

The protocol separates that question from transfer and robustness. The hypotheses are:

| ID | Hypothesis | Planned evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H1-N | Historical incomplete hypothesis: FFT magnitude contains useful signal after geometry-controlled 128 x 128 rasterisation. | The observed radial baseline and one RGB pilot are exploratory only; the matched neural comparison was intentionally stopped. |
| H1-HR | Under the frozen HighRes-v1 corpus and equal declared budgets, the tested representation has measurable internal association beyond named source strata. | Completed matched three-seed validation comparison, frozen representative checkpoint, parent-group bootstrap, and generator-stratified internal test. |
| H2 | A strong internal score does not by itself establish transfer to a new real-photo domain or to external generators with different relationships to training. | A frozen HighRes-v1 model and validation threshold evaluated on Synthbuster synthetic images against the RAISE-1k real-photo subset, reported per generator and training-relation category. |
| H3 | JPEG compression, resizing, and blur can change an observed signal. | Completed frozen-checkpoint evaluation under JPEG Q95/Q75/Q50, 0.75/0.50 resize, and Gaussian blur; no augmentation variant was selected after test inspection. |

H2 and H3 can fail even if a future HighRes-v1 internal result is strong. Such a result would
constrain the claim rather than invalidate the experiment.

## 1.4 Scientific novelty and practical value

This coursework does not claim a new state-of-the-art detector. Its contribution is methodological:
it makes dataset auditing, source-normalised preprocessing, grouped evaluation, and external
locking explicit in a compact reproducible workflow. In particular, the work records a discovered
geometry confound instead of keeping its attractive numerical result. This is practically important
because an interface that presents a shortcut as an AI detector could create unjustified confidence
in an incorrect label.

Section 4 specifies their status and the future high-resolution protocol. Section 5 reports only

# 2 Review of analogous works

## 2.1 Spatial neural classifiers

Convolutional neural networks are commonly used as image classifiers because they can learn spatial
patterns directly from pixels. ResNet introduced residual connections that make deep networks easier
to optimise [3]. A ResNet-50 is therefore a transparent, widely documented reference architecture
for a controlled representation comparison. It is not used here as evidence that a specific
architecture is inherently suitable for forensic authentication.

For AI-image detection, end-to-end RGB classifiers can exploit semantic, texture, and rendering
features. Their apparent strength depends on the training distribution. Ricker et al. found that
detectors developed for one generative family may not reliably recognise images from diffusion
models without retraining [4]. This is a reminder that the question “can a classifier separate this
training distribution?” is different from “can it identify images from an unknown generator?”

## 2.2 Frequency-domain evidence

The FFT represents an image by spatial-frequency components. For an image raster $x$, the proposed
FFT representation uses a shifted two-dimensional transform and log magnitude,

$$
F(x) = \log(1 + |\operatorname{fftshift}(\operatorname{FFT2}(x))|).
$$

The magnitude discards phase and, in this project, is calculated from a grayscale version of the
same standardised raster used by the RGB model. Frequency-domain approaches are motivated by prior
work reporting anomalous Fourier or spectral properties in synthetic images [5, 7]. However, an FFT
also preserves artifacts introduced by resizing, compression, or acquisition pipelines. A spectral
separation is therefore not automatically evidence of a generator-specific trace.

This trade-off motivates two safeguards in this study. First, direct rectangular-to-square resize is
prohibited: it could add label-correlated resampling frequencies before the FFT is taken. Second,
frequency and RGB models must receive the same raster and the same training budget. A gain can then
be interpreted as a difference between representations under the stated protocol, not merely as a
difference in image geometry.

## 2.3 Generalisation and robustness benchmarks

GenImage explicitly frames AI-image detection with cross-generator and degraded-image tasks [6].
These tasks matter because a detector can perform well when the evaluation images share generators
or preprocessing with training, but fail after compression, blur, downsampling, or a generator
change. The work therefore separates internal, external, and robustness evidence rather than
combining them into a single score.

Synthbuster is a complementary source of diffusion-generated imagery and an associated benchmark
for detector assessment [8]. It is not mixed into training or model selection in this project.
Instead, it will be paired with the separately licensed RAISE-1k real-photo subset [9] after the
HighRes-v1 internal design is frozen. This protects the external evaluation from inadvertent tuning.
Its generator relations to the frozen HighRes-v1 training manifest will be recorded as
same-named, same-family/different-version, same-family/version-unspecified, or unseen-family;
they must be reported separately rather than inferred from the historical Defactify pilot.

## 2.4 Research gap addressed by this work

This project treats related prompts, duplicate candidates, and file geometry crossing a held-out
split as explicit threats to validity. The risk is particularly important when each real source
image has multiple synthetic counterparts. The current project addresses it by:

- retaining the original Defactify split only as an audited benchmark reference;
- for HighRes-v1, forming connected leakage groups from documented parent/source links, exact file
  and pixel hashes, and perceptual-hash candidates before split assignment;
- allocating complete groups to one partition only;
- reporting a deliberately non-semantic metadata control as a bias diagnostic;
- using one source-normalised raster for every pixel model; and
- reserving an external corpus until the training and threshold rules have been fixed.

The emphasis is not on maximising a preliminary score, but on making a later result interpretable.

# 3 Collecting a dataset

## 3.1 HighRes-v1: primary study and current acquisition status

HighRes-v1 is a separate pre-registered study rather than an upscaled continuation of Defactify.
CommunityForensics-Small [10, 11] at revision
6c539a534c07917307c381f5af4053c6091b5278 was first audited as a candidate reservoir. Its complete
metadata catalog exposed a decisive confound: the strict jointly intended technical gate of 512 x
512, PNG, RGB, and explicitly non-NSFW images contains 1,005 real rows and 228,833 generated rows.
This source is therefore rejected for the general HighRes-v1 primary study. The gate is not loosened
to obtain a larger but geometry- or encoder-confounded corpus. No HighRes-v1 model has been trained
on this source.

The B-Free controlled comparison [13] remains a desirable later external or training ablation if
its official archives can be reached and verified. It was not substituted with an unofficial
repack and is not the materialised primary corpus reported here.

For a separate Defactify native384 sensitivity audit, one real and one fake were selected per
caption group, output crops were canonical 384 x 384 RGB PNGs, and the original upstream
train/validation/test roles were retained after excluding whole leakage components that crossed a
role. The final artifact contains 16,328 rows; it is not HighRes-v1 because source scale remains
label-correlated. A file-size-only control on its locked upstream test role gave ROC-AUC 0.650114
and balanced accuracy 0.595238. This rejects it as a positive training or model-selection corpus
while retaining it as reproducible negative data-quality evidence.

DANI [14], revision 870e29fcdc13c405fae35442899e9ba1da11691d, was first scanned through its seven
non-binary metadata fields only: index, declared size, category, class identifier, model,
generation type, and reference flag. A second Arrow projection requested `image.path` but not
`image.bytes`, recovering candidate parent-image and caption identifiers from locked basenames.
The offline mapping audit joined all 540,258 catalogue rows exactly to
`image_captions_dict_new.json` from D-Judge revision
6b877a12df94ddc4f68abb54db7912dc966d17e4 (file SHA-256
`d21590e888794de4faa768f2a36c3f00c8088fb23330bf0b1e1addd8437999e7`). It observed 5,000 candidate
parents and 25,014 candidate parent-caption pairs; every parent and every pair occurs under both
labels. This is evidence that the reconstructed keys agree with the pinned D-Judge mapping, not an
official COCO identity claim. The official COCO audit then matched all 5,000 filenames and all
25,014 caption ID/parent/text tuples exactly; every parent is from COCO `val2017`. The
licence/provenance decision allows only local non-commercial coursework use, attribution, and no
raw redistribution. To avoid unresolved NoDerivs, ShareAlike, or ambiguous records, the frozen
selection accepts only official Flickr licence IDs 2 and 4.

The final selection contains 1,482 eligible COCO parents and five cells per parent: `real_coco`,
`fake_dalle3_t2i`, `fake_sdxl_i2i`, `fake_sdxl_t2i`, and `fake_sdxl_ti2i`. Whole parents were
assigned to train, validation, and internal test, producing 5,185, 1,115, and 1,110 rows
respectively. Every decoded image is exactly 1024 x 1024. The first byte-level audit found zero
exact duplicate groups, zero cross-label exact duplicates, zero cross-parent pHash links at Hamming
distance at most four, and zero cross-split integrity components. It nevertheless failed closed
because real files supported JPEG and modes RGB/L, while generated files supported JPEG/PNG and
mode RGB.

The original 3.1 GB materialisation was retained unchanged. A deterministic derivative applies
EXIF orientation, converts decoded pixels to RGB, and stores an exact 1024 x 1024 PNG without
resizing. Its 8.2 GB local corpus includes a source-lineage CSV with original and canonical byte,
pixel, and perceptual hashes. A fresh audit found the same zero duplicate/leakage counts and uniform
PNG/RGB support, producing the training-manifest SHA-256
`3b88920920add51ef8c55b225817448b759c05a8d5bfc1cee11bc6befced2855`.

The common raster is `highres_square_crop_384_v1`: exact 1024 x 1024 source validation followed by
one 384 x 384 LANCZOS downsample. No upsampling or second source resize is permitted. The frozen
split and validation-only training rules were declared before neural performance was inspected.
Internal test and external corpora remain locked during model selection.

## 3.2 Historical low-resolution pilot: Defactify

The internal development corpus is the **Defactify Image Dataset** [1, 2]. It contains 96,000 records:
16,000 real MS COCO photographs and 16,000 synthetic images from each of five declared generators:
Stable Diffusion 2.1 (SD 2.1), SDXL, SD 3, DALL-E 3, and Midjourney v6. The source card reports an
official 42,000 / 9,000 / 45,000 train/validation/test split.

The local preparation process retains a manifest with image path, binary label, generator, original
split, group identifier, a local row identifier (`source_id`), caption, image geometry, canonical
file size, SHA-256 hash, and perceptual hash. The current Defactify preparation falls back to an
identifier of the form `split:index` when the source record has no ID, so this field is not treated
as provenance evidence or as a leakage link. Raw source files and image pixels are excluded from
version control. Images first have their EXIF orientation normalised and are then decoded to RGB;
other EXIF and container fields are not supplied to the neural-model input, but this does not erase
all pixel-level properties of the source corpus. License and source information must remain attached
to raw data and are not asserted to be commercially redistributable by this project.

## 3.3 Historical audit of the official Defactify partition

Before any classifier result was treated as evidence, the official split was audited for duplicate
and content relationships. The audit found 1,590 rows with a caption crossing partitions, 1,758
rows with a cross-partition group ID, and 81 rows with an **exact** cross-partition perceptual-hash
match. The local `source_id` value was not used as evidence in this audit because it is a unique
row identifier in this preparation. These facts make the official partition unsuitable as the
primary independence claim for this coursework, even though it may be useful for benchmark
replication.

The following geometry result was more consequential:

| Audit observation | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Real records | 16,000 photographs; 259 observed widths and 367 observed heights; median aspect ratio 1.33. |
| Synthetic records | 80,000 images; every one is square. |
| Exact width-height pair shared by both labels | None. |
| Consequence | Direct resizing of every image to a square can introduce a label-correlated resampling trace. |

This observation is not a flaw that can be repaired by reporting a higher metric. It changes what a
model may be learning. The correction is therefore embedded in the preprocessing protocol and the
interpretation rules, rather than applied as a post-hoc explanation.

## 3.4 Historical Defactify group-disjoint split

A custom split was generated by taking connected components over `group_id`, exact image SHA-256,
and **exactly equal** perceptual hashes. Each connected component, called `leakage_group`, was
assigned to exactly one of train, validation, or test. A deterministic seed of 20260829 and a
greedy allocation target of 70/15/15 were used to retain equal counts for every declared source.

The resulting manifest has SHA-256
`695a1442badc6a22ec4d3d4324592df5aa781f6d83e33e3323e8d1fb6d758225`. It contains 9,826
connected components, with largest component size 84. The source manifest checksum is
`7972e820c74894312fa448a43626ad3c4306c90e2b7b9dac46d32484d6b006ce`.

| Split | Images | Real | AI | Per declared source (real, SD 2.1, SDXL, SD 3, DALL-E 3, Midjourney v6) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Train | 67,200 | 11,200 | 56,000 | 11,200 each |
| Validation | 14,400 | 2,400 | 12,000 | 2,400 each |
| Test | 14,400 | 2,400 | 12,000 | 2,400 each |
| Total | 96,000 | 16,000 | 80,000 | 16,000 each |

The post-split audit recorded zero cross-split matches for normalized captions/group IDs, exact
SHA-256 hashes, and exactly equal pHash values. It did **not** perform a Hamming-distance pHash
candidate scan or semantic near-duplicate search, so it cannot prove that every visual or content
relationship is absent. This makes the split more appropriate for an internal stress test, but it
does not remove the broader limitation that all images originate from one benchmark construction.

## 3.5 Locked and conditional external sources

The confirmatory evaluation is specified as Synthbuster synthetic images compared with the
RAISE-1k real-photo subset [9]. It must remain absent from HighRes-v1 training, early stopping,
threshold choice, augmentation choice, architecture choice, and seed selection. The local
preparation utility does not automatically download it: this keeps the benchmark locked and
requires the operator to observe RAISE's stated non-commercial research and educational-use terms.
Generator relationships will be classified against the frozen HighRes-v1 manifest rather than
assumed from Defactify. No pooled external score may be called an “unseen-generator” result. At the
snapshot date, no external performance result is available.

CommunityForensics-Eval [12] remains conditional and is not a training or selection source. Before
any score, it requires an exact- and perceptual-hash contamination audit against the materialised
HighRes-v1 corpus and adherence to its source-specific FFHQ/COCO split restrictions. A passed check
would support a clearly labelled cross-dataset result; a failed or inconclusive check excludes it.

## 4.2 Historical H1-N common rasterisation

The historical H1-N preprocessing was fixed as follows:

1. Normalise EXIF orientation, decode the file as RGB, and do not pass other EXIF or
   original-container fields to the model.
2. Crop a square without padding. H1-N **neural** training uses a seed-controlled random square
   crop keyed by the experiment seed, epoch, and stable `source_id`, followed by a train-only
   horizontal flip with probability 0.5. Neural validation, internal test, robustness, and external
   evaluation use a deterministic centre crop. The completed radial logistic baseline extracts one
   fixed feature vector per image, so it uses the deterministic centre crop and no augmentation on
   train, validation, and test.
3. Resize the crop once to 128 x 128 pixels using LANCZOS interpolation. The source corpus has a
   minimum short side of 128 pixels, so this step does not require upsampling.
4. Supply this same standardised raster to the RGB model, or compute FFT magnitude only from that
   raster.

Letterboxing is prohibited because borders would reintroduce aspect ratio as an observable channel.
No pixel model receives width, height, aspect ratio, source file size, format, caption, generator
name, group ID, or other manifest metadata.

## 4.3 Pre-registered HighRes-v1 data and compute contract

HighRes-v1 uses the identifier `highres_square_crop_384_v1`, rather than an H1-N alias. The
canonical DANI source has already been EXIF-normalised and stored as exact 1024 x 1024 RGB PNG.
The loader verifies the exact source geometry and makes one common 384 x 384 LANCZOS raster before
RGB or FFT representation processing. It rejects a smaller substitute rather than upsampling it.

The initial Apple MPS device benchmark established that a scratch ResNet-50 training step at 384
pixels is feasible within the requested unified-memory budget: batch 64 sustained approximately
43 images/s with about 20.4 GB Metal driver allocation, while batch 80 used about 25.6 GB without
material throughput gain. Initial HighRes-v1 work will therefore start at batch 64. This is a
hardware-readiness observation, not a classifier-performance result.

The practical series is frozen as ImageNet-pretrained RGB ResNet-50 for seeds 7, 17, and 42, batch
64, learning rate 1e-4, at most 15 epochs, patience 4, train-only horizontal flip, and one-real/
one-fake parent-paired sampling. Validation log loss selects each checkpoint and validation
balanced accuracy selects its threshold. All seeds are reported; the numerically best seed is not
substituted for the predeclared representative. A separate representation hypothesis compares RGB
and FFT ResNet-50 from random initialisation with equal budgets, avoiding an unmatched RGB
pretraining prior. Internal test is not constructed during these launches.

## 4.4 Historical H1-N models and equal-budget plan

The historical experimental ladder was deliberately small and interpretable:

1. Majority and random sanity checks.
2. A 64-bin radial log-power spectrum followed by standardisation and class-balanced logistic
   regression. This provides a transparent pixel-only baseline.
3. RGB ResNet-50 from random initialisation.
4. FFT-magnitude ResNet-50 from random initialisation. The grayscale log magnitude is robustly
   scaled per image and repeated into three channels solely to meet the backbone input shape.

The planned historical neural comparison used the same ResNet-50 architecture, input size, optimiser family,
learning-rate schedule, batch size, epoch cap, early-stopping policy, paired group sampler, and
validation-only threshold rule. Both were to be trained from random initialisation. ImageNet
pretraining was not used in this historical planned comparison because it supplies a semantic RGB
prior for which there is no equivalent FFT interpretation. It may be studied later only as a
separately labelled practical ablation.

Each historical controlled training epoch uses a paired group sampler. For every group visit, it samples one
real image and one synthetic sibling, selecting synthetic generators uniformly from available
siblings. This avoids allowing very large groups or the five-to-one AI/real prevalence to dominate
the training signal. The predeclared seeds were 7, 17, and 42; the remaining runs were intentionally
stopped and no best seed may be selected from the test set.

For this historical protocol, the launcher fail-closes if the grouped manifest has a
cross-split exact overlap in `leakage_group`, raw `group_id`, `source_id`, SHA-256, pHash, or a
non-empty caption. It also records the manifest hash, preprocessing contract, architecture,
initialisation choice, augmentation flag, sampler, device, and launch environment. The unexecuted
seed aggregation would have required these records to agree and pinned the SHA-256 of the fixed
seed-17 candidate checkpoint.

## 4.5 Historical H1-N selection, metrics, and uncertainty

Only validation predictions selected an early-stopping checkpoint and threshold; the threshold
maximised validation balanced accuracy. The internal test was never used for threshold selection.
Because D0 had already exposed that test split, amended H1-N test results remain **exploratory**;
they cannot serve as a confirmatory result.

The following was the historical unexecuted selection rule. For each representation, validation
balanced accuracy would have been averaged over seeds 7, 17, and 42. The representation with the
larger mean would have been the prototype candidate; an exact tie would have been resolved by mean
validation ROC-AUC and then by the lexicographically earlier representation name. The candidate
checkpoint would have been the predeclared seed-17 run of that representation, not its numerically
best seed; if it were unavailable, the prototype would have stayed disabled. This rule is not
transferred to HighRes-v1.

The primary metrics are ROC-AUC, balanced accuracy, and macro-F1. Accuracy is secondary because
the corpus has 12,000 synthetic and 2,400 real test images, so an all-synthetic prediction would
already yield high plain accuracy. Secondary reporting includes class recall, precision, PR-AUC,
FPR@TPR95, Brier score, expected calibration error (ECE), confusion matrix, and a paired
group-ranking measure. FPR@TPR95 is a descriptive operating point recomputed from the scored test
ROC curve, not performance at the validation-selected decision threshold. It is particularly
important because a high true-positive target can otherwise conceal an unacceptably high rate of
falsely flagging real images.

Confidence intervals use 2,000 percentile bootstrap resamples of complete `leakage_group`
clusters. This avoids pretending that several images linked by content/prompt are independent.
For model-versus-model comparisons, both prediction tables must contain exactly the same image paths
and groups, and the comparison bootstraps the metric difference at the group level.

## 4.6 HighRes-v1 robustness and external evaluation rules

HighRes-v1 robustness conditions applied deterministic JPEG qualities 95, 75, and 50, resizing by
0.75 and 0.50, and Gaussian blur in the predeclared evaluation path. Results list each condition
separately and are not folded into a clean-score average. Because the test has now been opened, a
robust-augmentation variant cannot be selected from these outcomes and is not introduced post hoc.

For external evaluation, the HighRes-v1 architecture, preprocessing, selected checkpoint rule,
seed aggregation, and validation threshold must be frozen. The external report will contrast all
RAISE real images with each Synthbuster generator separately, followed by the predeclared
aggregation and worst-generator values. It will additionally stratify the frozen training-relation
categories rather than relabel the whole corpus as unseen. External metrics are a fixed transfer
check, not an opportunity to replace the selected representation or seed. This design tests
transfer to a new real-photo source and a range of generator relationships, not merely a larger
test set.

# 5 Results and discussion

## 5.1 D0 diagnostic controls: a result that limits the claim

The original group-disjoint split was used to diagnose potentially non-semantic shortcuts. Table 1
shows that a model receiving only canonical PNG width, height, aspect ratio, and byte size achieved
a stronger score than the legacy radial FFT baseline. The file-metadata model is not an image
detector and was never a candidate for the interface.

**Table 1. D0 diagnostics on the grouped Defactify internal test (n = 14,400).**

| Diagnostic run | Input / preprocessing | ROC-AUC | BAcc | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| File-metadata logistic control, seed 7 | Width, height, aspect ratio, canonical PNG byte size | 0.9807239 | 0.9758333 | Evidence of a powerful dataset/source shortcut; not a detector result. |
| Legacy radial FFT logistic, seed 7 | Direct rectangular-to-square resize before radial FFT | 0.8161259 | 0.7477083 | Confounded by geometry-dependent resampling; retained as D0 only. |

The control makes the causal concern concrete. All synthetic records are square, whereas many real
records are rectangular. Directly forcing the latter into a square can leave frequency patterns
that correspond to resampling, not synthesis. The high D0 values therefore cannot support
statements such as “the method detects AI images,” even on the internal corpus. They instead justify
the H1-N correction.

## 5.5 What the historical Defactify results do and do not show

The controlled radial baseline shows that, after a visible geometry shortcut is removed, a
frequency-domain statistic still has some discriminatory association within the specified benchmark.
Together with the one RGB pilot, it does **not** show whether FFT is better than RGB: the model
class, training procedure, and one-seed status differ, and there is no matched FFT neural result.
The historical Defactify evidence does **not** show robustness to transformations; H3 is evaluated
only in the later DANI study. It does **not** show transfer to a new real-photo source or to the
external generator-relation categories, because H2 remains unevaluated.

The correct current conclusion is therefore modest: the historical observations motivate a new
high-resolution, leakage-audited study, while the large D0 shortcut and generator variation
demonstrate why a single internal score would be insufficient.

The controlled neural crop and flip sequence is portable across a relocation of the repository
because it is keyed by the stable manifest `source_id`, not an absolute file path. Nevertheless,
Apple MPS is recorded as the training device and PyTorch requests deterministic algorithms with a
warning fallback; this supports repeatable protocol execution but does not justify a claim that
independent neural reruns are bitwise identical. Near-duplicates that are not connected by the
declared `group_id`, exact SHA-256, or exact pHash remain a residual limitation of the split audit.

## 5.6 DANI data-gate and validation-only results

The DANI image-byte audit is a completed result about the corpus, not about classifier quality. Its
7,410 original rows contained no exact or near-duplicate leakage under the declared checks, but
the unequal JPEG/PNG and L/RGB support correctly blocked training. After lossless source-raster
canonicalisation, the repeated audit passed with uniform RGB PNG support and produced a frozen
training manifest.

The acquisition-metadata control quantifies the shortcut instead of discarding it. On validation,
the original files yielded ROC-AUC 0.713427, balanced accuracy 0.660314, and macro-F1 0.552612.
The canonical files yielded ROC-AUC 0.537373, balanced accuracy 0.573430, and macro-F1 0.509027.
The remaining weak association may reflect canonical PNG size/content correlations; it is a
dataset-bias warning and is unavailable to the pixel model as an explicit feature. The internal
test was not evaluated for either control.

The deterministic 64-bin radial log-power FFT baseline used the same 384 x 384 common raster and
no file metadata. On validation it obtained ROC-AUC 0.862972, balanced accuracy 0.803251,
macro-F1 0.810515, FPR@TPR95 0.376682, and 15-bin ECE 0.170572. Its improvement over the canonical
metadata control supports the hypothesis that the pixels contain useful frequency-domain signal.
Its high false-positive rate at high recall and poor calibration also show that radial power alone
is not a sufficient operational detector. The threshold was selected on validation, and internal
test was not read.

The one-epoch MPS pilot is excluded because it was declared solely to test GPU allocation, data
loading, checkpoint writing, and validation-only execution. All three predeclared practical runs

# 6 Prototype inference interface

## 6.1 Purpose and boundary

A small local web interface is included as a research-facing prototype, not as a public
authentication service. Its purpose is to make a frozen experimental checkpoint reproducibly
callable on one uploaded image after a model has passed the complete HighRes-v1 selection process.
A validation-selected and internally evaluated checkpoint now exists, but it failed common
robustness conditions and has no external transfer result. The inference gate therefore remains
closed: the interface must not promote it to an authentication service or a deployable detector.

## 6.2 Intended operation after selection

When a completed experiment is explicitly selected, the local service should:

1. Require an `AI_IMAGE_DETECTOR_SELECTION_RECORD` JSON record with schema
   `ai_image_detector_model_selection_v1`, `frozen_external_validated` status, the intended
   experiment directory, a checkpoint SHA-256, and the recorded architecture/preprocessing contract;
   reject a checkpoint whose state dictionary or hash is incompatible.
2. Decode the upload safely, reject malformed, excessively large, or decompression-bomb images,
   and convert it to RGB.
3. Apply exactly the evaluation crop and rasterisation recorded in the experiment, rather than a
   separate user-interface transform.
4. Return the raw model score, validation-selected decision rule, representation, experiment name,
   and checkpoint checksum.
5. State visibly that the result is experimental and does not establish provenance.

The implementation is local and does not retain uploaded images. It refuses to start inference
without the hash-pinned, human-supplied selection record; the selected run must belong to a
completed HighRes-v1 experiment with an auditable manifest, preprocessing, internal evidence, and
separately inspectable external-validation record. Historical H1-N and diagnostic artifacts are
explicitly ineligible. This protects against accidentally serving a smoke checkpoint, a legacy run,
or an incomplete training run as if it were a research result. The record is an auditable launch
gate, not independent evidence that an external evaluation was actually performed; that evidence
must still be inspected in its own artifacts.

## 6.3 Interface language and non-claims

The interface must say **“model score”** and, if a thresholded display is enabled, **“AI-like under
this experimental model”** or **“not AI-like under this experimental model.”** It must not say
“authentic,” “fake,” “AI-generated with X% probability,” or “proven.” The result must be accompanied
by the tested generators, known source/geometry limitations, external-validation status, and a
warning that re-encoding, editing, cropping, or a new/shifted generator may change the score.

Public deployment is out of scope for this research snapshot. Serving a neural checkpoint publicly
would require a separately selected inference environment, security review, rate limits, privacy
policy, and evidence stronger than the current exploratory result.

# 7 Conclusion

The coursework set out to test whether frequency-domain information improves experimental
real-versus-synthetic image classification. Its main completed contribution is a reproducible
audit-and-control workflow that changed the original experimental interpretation. The Defactify
data contain a material class-geometry difference: all synthetic images are square while real
photographs have varied aspect ratios. A metadata-only logistic control achieved ROC-AUC 0.9807239
and balanced accuracy 0.9758333, showing that source properties alone can create an apparently
excellent result. The legacy radial FFT score of ROC-AUC 0.8161259 and BAcc 0.7477083 must
therefore remain a D0 diagnostic, not a detector claim.

After amending preprocessing to use a common source-normalised 128 x 128 raster, the controlled
radial FFT baseline obtained ROC-AUC 0.7088641, BAcc 0.6538333, and macro-F1 0.5875769 on the
14,400-image group-disjoint internal test. The result is exploratory, has a high FPR@TPR95, is
uncalibrated, and varies substantially between synthetic generators. It demonstrates neither a
general detector nor superiority of FFT over RGB.

The completed 128 x 128 evidence consists of diagnostic controls, a controlled radial FFT baseline,
and one MPS-recorded RGB ResNet-50 pilot. These are historical exploratory results on the grouped
Defactify benchmark. They neither complete the original RGB-versus-FFT comparison nor select a
deployable model, and they cannot be relabelled as high-resolution evidence.

DANI passed lineage, licence, materialisation, duplicate, shortcut-normalisation, and split-isolation
gates. The original-file metadata control showed measurable validation association, and its
reduction after canonicalisation demonstrates why container support must be separated from pixel
evidence. This is a genuine negative-and-corrective data result rather than a reason to discard the
corpus silently.

The equal-initialisation RGB/FFT comparison is complete. Across validation seeds 7, 17, and 42,
FFT achieved higher mean balanced accuracy than RGB (0.787556 versus 0.657138) and was selected by
the frozen rule. The predeclared representative seed-17 checkpoint obtained internal-test ROC-AUC
0.893941, balanced accuracy 0.826014, and macro-F1 0.774670 with parent-group uncertainty. This is
a real internal academic result, not a pilot estimate.

The result is nevertheless not suitable for deployment. Performance varied by generator condition,
and common post-processing caused severe failures. Balanced accuracy fell close to or below chance
for JPEG Q50, both resize conditions, and Gaussian blur. Because these values were seen only after
selection, the project does not retrain or change the threshold in response. The negative
robustness result is part of the conclusion rather than an inconvenience to be hidden.

External Synthbuster + RAISE transfer remains not evaluated because that separately licensed
corpus was not materialised locally. Consequently, the work supports a narrow statement: a frozen
FFT ResNet-50 learned an internally reproducible association in the audited DANI distribution, but
the association is brittle and has not been shown to transfer. The local interface remains gated,
and no score is described as a probability, proof of provenance, or universal AI detector.

# Appendix D. Interpretation checklist for any future result

Before a future score is included in the final coursework conclusion or exposed in an interface,
the following questions must be answered affirmatively:

1. Was the model selected without inspecting the relevant test or external test labels?
2. Did RGB and FFT receive the same documented rasterisation and matched training budget?
3. Were known normalized-caption/group-ID, exact-SHA-256, and exact-pHash components kept in one
   partition, with any remaining near-duplicate risk stated?
4. Are the reported thresholds validation-selected and distinct from test metrics?
5. Are uncertainty intervals resampled by content/leakage group?
6. Are all predeclared seeds reported, rather than only the strongest one?
7. Are external generator-relation and transformation results reported separately from internal
   performance?
8. Does the wording call the output a model score and state the benchmark limits?
9. If a series is incomplete or intentionally stopped, is it labelled as a pilot and excluded from
   model selection?

If any answer is no, the result must be described as a diagnostic or pilot observation rather than
as evidence of general AI-image detection.
